# Distinguishing authority and coercion

Authority and coercion can both produce compliance, but they do so through different relations. Coercion secures behavior through threatened or applied force. Authority secures recognition without needing continuous force or fresh persuasion. The concepts are not simple opposites: an institution may rely on a background capacity for coercion while particular authoritative commands are followed because the office is recognized as legitimate.

The boundary becomes visible when recognition collapses. Increasing force can preserve obedience for a time, but that does not demonstrate increasing authority; it may show the opposite. Still, a single use of force does not by itself prove that all authority has disappeared. A careful explanation must therefore distinguish the basis of compliance from the instruments available to an institution.
