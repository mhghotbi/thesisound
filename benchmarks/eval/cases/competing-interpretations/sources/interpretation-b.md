# Interpretation B

The second interpretation agrees that plurality and participation are central, but it treats the thesis less as a direct political ideal and more as a diagnosis of modern categories. It emphasizes the instability of action, the dependence of public life on material supports, and the danger of romanticizing historical public spaces. Institutions do more than preserve a stage; they distribute access and shape whose speech becomes intelligible.

This reading does not reject public freedom. It disputes the first interpretation's tendency to detach freedom from labor, care, and exclusion. Its evidence is the tension between the formal account of equality and the historical limits of actual political membership.
