# Interpretation A

The first interpretation treats the thesis primarily as a theory of public freedom. It emphasizes scenes in which people act together without a predetermined outcome and argues that political freedom exists in the performance itself. Institutions are valuable when they preserve a space for repeated participation, but they cannot substitute for action. This reading acknowledges social and economic constraints, yet it warns that making administration the definition of politics removes initiative and contest from view.

Its strongest evidence is the repeated contrast between rule, fabrication, and action. The interpretation therefore reads the thesis normatively, as a defense of practices that let citizens appear before one another as equals.
