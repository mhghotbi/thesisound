# Primary argument

The text distinguishes action from labor and work. Labor answers recurring biological necessity, while work produces a relatively durable world of objects. Action instead occurs directly among plural persons and discloses who they are through words and deeds. The distinction is analytical rather than a claim that ordinary life separates these activities cleanly. Action is fragile because its consequences cannot be fully predicted or reversed, so institutions and promises matter without eliminating plurality.

The argument does not say that action is always morally good. It says that action is the specifically political capacity to begin something new with others. Any summary that turns this into individual self-expression loses the dependence on a shared public world.
