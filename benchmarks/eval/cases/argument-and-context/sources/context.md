# Historical context

The conceptual vocabulary responds to experiences of industrial mass society, totalitarian domination, and the shrinking of durable public spaces. The historical setting helps explain why the author resists reducing politics to administration or economic management. Yet the context should qualify rather than replace the conceptual argument: the categories are presented as tools for examining human activities, not as a chronological history of one regime.

A critical reading should preserve the tension between the value placed on public action and the exclusions that historically shaped many public realms. That criticism challenges the scope of the account without erasing its distinction between political appearance and private necessity.
