# Step 01 — Source Audit and Disagreement Inventory

Date: 2026-08-17
Status: completed
Scope: compare the three unverified agent syntheses before external verification or canonical taxonomy decisions.

## Purpose

This step does **not** decide the final field map. It identifies: (1) cross-agent convergence, (2) cases where the same phenomenon is classified at different levels, (3) genuine taxonomy disputes to resolve in Step 02, and (4) empirical/canonicality claims that must wait for Step 03 verification.

The goal is to avoid a false synthesis produced by averaging agent outputs.

## Source roles

### Batch 01 — broad candidate inventory
Best for recall: many candidate concepts, actors, mechanisms, and Global-South/Iran-oriented hypotheses. Weakest where an attractive phrase is promoted into a literature category without demonstrating canonicality. Use it to avoid omissions, not to determine hierarchy.

### Batch 02 — causal/taxonomic decomposition
Best for conceptual discrimination. Its strongest move is separating political economy, infrastructure, dependency, sovereignty, and geopolitical coercion because they ask different causal questions. Use it as the strongest candidate architecture, but its bibliographic/canonicality claims still require verification.

### Batch 03 — alternate field map / challenge set
Useful as an adversarial comparison to Batch 02. It recombines some areas Batch 02 separates, foregrounds labor/materiality, and treats military-tech as cross-cutting. It also introduces current quantitative concentration claims, but those should not affect taxonomy until independently verified.

# A. High-confidence convergence across the three inputs

These are cross-agent agreements, not externally verified findings.

## A1. There is no single literature that covers the whole problem

All three inputs converge on a plural field rather than one coherent canon called `AI colonialism`.

Recurring literatures/families:
- Political Economy of AI / digital capitalism
- STS / Critical AI / Critical Data Studies
- Postcolonial / Decolonial technology studies
- Data / Digital Colonialism
- Material / infrastructure analysis of AI
- IPE / geopolitics / Weaponized Interdependence
- Digital / Technological / AI Sovereignty
- Indigenous Data Sovereignty / collective data governance
- Public / commons-based / institutional alternatives

**Step-02 implication:** the canonical map should be multi-literature; `Decolonial AI` should not be assumed to be the universal parent.

## A2. Diagnosis and alternatives live partly in different literatures

All inputs support a qualified version of the working hypothesis:
- decolonial/postcolonial and data-colonial work is especially strong in historical, epistemic, relational, and extractive diagnosis;
- capital-intensive institutional/technical alternatives are dispersed across public infrastructure, industrial policy, commons/data governance, competition/interoperability, and public-policy literatures;
- Indigenous Data Governance is an important counterexample because it combines critique with relatively concrete governance principles.

**Implication:** the final map should encode `diagnostic role` and `alternative-building role` separately.

## A3. Sovereignty must not be equated with state control

All three distinguish state/national sovereignty from community/Indigenous/public/commons-oriented control or self-determination.

Recurring warning:
- replacing foreign corporate control with domestic state/security control is not automatically decolonization;
- jurisdiction/localization is not identical to technological capability.

**Implication:** sovereignty should later be decomposed by **object** (data, compute, cloud, technology, AI stack) and **subject/actor** (state, region, community, Indigenous nation, public institution, individual, commons).

## A4. AI dependency is layered

A recurring stack is:

`minerals / energy → chips → compute → cloud → models → data → labor → applications/platforms`

Batch 02 additionally foregrounds `standards / governance` and software/interface dependencies.

**Implication:** keep the `AI stack` separate from the `literature map`. The stack tells us **where power/dependency occurs**; the literature map tells us **which tradition explains which mechanism**.

## A5. Colonial terminology is contested

All inputs require distinctions among colonialism, coloniality, postcolonial, decolonial, data colonialism, digital colonialism, algorithmic colonization/coloniality, and extractivism. They also agree that foreign corporate power or dependency is not automatically colonialism.

**Implication:** Step 02 needs a taxonomic place for colonial/decolonial analysis, while Step 03 must verify conceptual boundaries and critiques.

## A6. `Global South` is too coarse as a causal category

Across the inputs it appears in distinct relations:
- data source
- outsourced labor pool
- mineral/resource site
- linguistic/epistemic periphery
- cloud/model-dependent market
- geopolitically vulnerable state
- regional/sub-hegemonic power
- site of alternative governance/capability building

**Implication:** Global South should be a comparative/case-selection dimension, not a homogeneous actor or single field cluster.

## A7. Open source is not equivalent to sovereignty

Recurring agreement:
- open weights may reduce model/API dependency;
- compute, chips, software stack, training data, deployment infrastructure, expertise, or distribution may remain concentrated.

Likewise, public ownership is not automatically democratic governance, and decentralization is not automatically anti-monopoly.

# B. Genuine taxonomy disputes to resolve in Step 02

## B1. Taxonomic status of Data / Digital Colonialism

- **Batch 01:** major standalone cluster and sometimes near-parent frame for labor/epistemic extraction.
- **Batch 02:** recognizable, contested literature distinct enough to map, but not a universal parent.
- **Batch 03:** more strongly treats it as an interface/subfield between Political Economy and Decolonial/Postcolonial theory.

**Resolution required:** first-order literature, second-order subfield/interface, or concept family nested under Critical Data Studies?

**Decision criterion:** intellectual lineage + distinct central question + independent canon, not public prominence.

## B2. Political Economy and Infrastructure: one cluster or two?

- **Batch 01:** combines them as `Materiality & Political Economy of AI`.
- **Batch 02:** separates ownership/accumulation/rent/IP from cloud/compute/chips/material-organizational dependency.
- **Batch 03:** partially recombines them under `Political Economy of AI / Platform & Infrastructural Power`, while materiality also appears in Critical Data/AI + STS.

**Resolution required:** does `Infrastructure Studies / Material AI` have distinct explanatory logic and canon sufficient for a first-order node?

**Provisional internal judgment:** Batch 02's separation is methodologically cleaner because ownership power and infrastructural dependence are different mechanisms. Not yet canonical.

## B3. Dependency Theory / technological capability as a first-order cluster

- **Batch 01:** mostly folds dependency into `Digital Sovereignty & Technological Dependency`.
- **Batch 02:** elevates `Dependency, Development & Technological Capability` to a distinct literature with its own genealogy and causal question.
- **Batch 03:** uses dependency throughout but distributes it across political economy, IPE, and sovereignty.

**Why it matters:** if dependency is not separated from sovereignty, the map risks jumping directly from concentration/power to response/sovereignty without explaining how asymmetric capability is reproduced.

**Step-02 test:** does dependency literature contribute a mechanism irreducible to market concentration, infrastructural lock-in, geopolitical coercion, or sovereignty policy?

## B4. Labor: field cluster, material substrate, or cross-cutting axis?

- **Batch 01:** standalone `Global Division of AI Labor / Ghost Work` cluster.
- **Batch 02:** labor is a material substrate cutting across power dimensions, with a neighboring sociology/digital-labor literature.
- **Batch 03:** labor/ghost work is central but cross-cutting rather than one of the eight executive clusters.

**Resolution required:** separate whether `Digital Labor / sociology of AI labor` is an independent literature from whether labor deserves a first-order node in this specific map.

## B5. Epistemic / linguistic power: cluster or dimension?

- **Batch 01:** standalone `Epistemic & Linguistic Imperialism` cluster.
- **Batch 02:** interface among Decolonial AI, Critical Data Studies, and NLP; also one of four power dimensions.
- **Batch 03:** primarily inside Decolonial/Postcolonial theory, with epistemic/linguistic power proposed as a level.

**Resolution required:** do not mix `literature` and `mechanism`. Linguistic marginalization may be a major mechanism/site without being a standalone intellectual tradition.

## B6. Military-tech nexus: cluster or cross-cutting interface?

- **Batch 01:** preserves military/security concerns but does not establish a stable peer literature node.
- **Batch 02:** interface among Security Studies, military innovation, political economy, and infrastructure.
- **Batch 03:** explicitly a central cross-cutting axis between Political Economy and IPE/Security.

**Provisional internal judgment:** cross-cutting treatment is structurally cleaner than making military-tech a peer of broad traditions such as Political Economy or Decolonial Theory. Canonicality still needs Step 03.

## B7. Standards / private rule-making: central omission or secondary topic?

- **Batch 01:** mostly absent.
- **Batch 02:** important cross-cutting literature shaping interoperability, lock-in, market access, technical defaults, and international influence.
- **Batch 03:** not promoted into the executive eight-cluster map.

**Resolution required:** cross-cutting mechanism layer, first-order literature, or subtopic within infrastructure/geopolitics/governance?

Because standards can produce dependency without ownership, they cannot simply be ignored.

## B8. Four dimensions of power or five?

### Batch 02 — four mechanisms/forms
1. economic / ownership
2. infrastructural / dependency
3. geopolitical / coercive
4. epistemic / constitutive

Labor, minerals, and energy are treated as material substrates.

### Batch 03 — five-level challenge
Adds labor + material/ecological as a distinct level.

### Core issue
`ownership`, `dependency`, `coercion`, and `epistemic constitution` are mechanisms/forms of power. `labor`, `minerals`, and `energy` are primarily sites/material substrates.

**Provisional internal judgment:** the four-dimensional model is conceptually cleaner if classifying **mechanisms of power**. Labor/materiality should then be represented orthogonally as sites/layers. Step 02 must decide the ontology explicitly.

# C. Apparent disagreements that are mostly level-of-analysis problems

## C1. Labor can be both a literature and a site
`Digital Labor / sociology of work` can be a neighboring literature while `labor` is simultaneously one layer in the AI production stack.

## C2. Language can be both an empirical domain and a mechanism
`low-resource NLP` is an empirical/technical domain; `linguistic coloniality` is a theoretical interpretation. Neither automatically needs to be a first-order field cluster.

## C3. Public Compute can be a policy family without being a discipline
`Public Compute / Public AI` can be an alternative-design family fed by public policy, industrial policy, commons, public administration, and infrastructure studies.

## C4. Military integration can be a cross-cutting phenomenon
The military-tech nexus may be studied through multiple literatures and need not become a peer literature itself.

### Key lesson
The final artifact probably needs more than one map:
1. **Literature map** — intellectual traditions and explanatory questions.
2. **AI stack / sites map** — chips, compute, cloud, models, data, labor, platforms, standards.
3. **Power-mechanism map** — ownership, dependency, coercion, epistemic/constitutive power.
4. **Response/alternative map** — sovereignty, public provision, commons, openness, interoperability, capability building.

Trying to put all four ontologies into one tree is the main source of inconsistency across the agents.

# E. Step-01 conclusion

The main result is a methodological correction:

> **The three agents mix four different classification dimensions: intellectual literatures, sites/layers of the AI stack, mechanisms/forms of power, and families of responses/alternatives.**

Most apparent disagreement comes from mixing those dimensions.

Therefore Step 02 should not vote between competing eight-cluster lists. It should define a clean ontology first and then place each concept into the correct dimension.

## Blocking questions for Step 02

1. What counts as a first-order **literature/tradition**?
2. Should `Dependency / Development / Technological Capability` be first-order?
3. Is `Data/Digital Colonialism` first-order, second-order, or a concept family/interface?
4. Should `Infrastructure / Material AI` be separated from Political Economy?
5. Where do Digital Labor and linguistic/epistemic research sit in the literature map versus the site/mechanism maps?
6. Where do Standards Studies and military-tech belong?
7. Which ontology should organize canonical `field-map.md`, and which dimensions should be represented as overlays rather than sibling branches?
