## Canonicalization rule

The previous field map mixed intellectual traditions, empirical layers, mechanisms of power, and proposed responses as if they were sibling clusters. The canonical map therefore uses five linked views:

- `L` — literatures / intellectual traditions
- `S` — AI stack / empirical sites
- `P` — power mechanisms
- `R` — response / alternative families
- `C` — case / geopolitical positions

This is the central structural decision of Step 04.

## Literature-map decisions

### Retained as first-order established or justified literature/problem families

1. Political Economy of Digital / AI Capitalism
2. Critical sociotechnical family: STS + Critical Data Studies + emerging Critical AI Studies
3. AI Infrastructure, Materiality & Political Ecology — explicitly labeled a composite problem family, not one bounded discipline
4. Postcolonial / Decolonial Technology Studies & Computing
5. Dependency / Development / Technological Capability — historical lineage established; AI application marked emerging
6. IPE / Geoeconomics / Security of Technology
7. Digital / Technological / Data Sovereignty & Strategic Autonomy — established but contested; AI/Compute Sovereignty marked emerging
8. Indigenous Data Sovereignty / Community Data Governance

### Not represented as a peer discipline

`Public AI / Public Compute / Commons / Industrial Alternatives` is retained as a dedicated response umbrella and expanded in `R`; it is not described as one coherent academic discipline.

## Interface decisions

- **Data Colonialism / Digital Colonialism**: established concept-families / subliteratures; kept as a named interface across Political Economy, Critical Data/STS, and Decolonial/Postcolonial technology studies.
- **Digital Extractivism**: adjacent vocabulary; not promoted into a verified independent canon.
- **Standards / private rule-making**: strengthened as a cross-cutting governance interface because standard-setting can shape interoperability, lock-in, access, and geopolitical influence without direct ownership.
- **Military / national-security technology nexus**: cross-cutting interface among Political Economy, Infrastructure, IPE/Security, and coercive power; not a top-level discipline.
- **Digital labor / AI labor**: neighboring literature plus central empirical site; not a peer node in the literature map.
- **Language / epistemic inequality**: empirical site plus epistemic mechanism; not a standalone top-level field.

## Power-model decisions

Retain four mechanism families, with coercion split internally:

- P1 economic / ownership / appropriative power
- P2 infrastructural / dependency power
- P3a external / geopolitical coercion
- P3b internal administrative / security coercion
- P4 epistemic / constitutive power

No P5 is created for labor/materiality because labor, minerals, energy, chips, data centers, language, etc. are primarily sites/substrates through which mechanisms operate.

## Critical conceptual distinctions preserved

The canonical map explicitly separates:

- concentration ≠ dependency
- dependency ≠ vulnerability
- vulnerability ≠ coercibility
- coercibility ≠ sovereignty/autonomy
- access to technology ≠ endogenous capability
- open weights ≠ open source AI
- open source ≠ public infrastructure
- public ownership ≠ democratic governance
- localization ≠ sovereignty
- state sovereignty ≠ Indigenous/community self-determination
- colonialism ≠ coloniality
- foreign corporate dominance ≠ automatically colonialism
