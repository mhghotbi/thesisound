## G. High-value hypotheses to verify later

1. Decolonial/postcolonial AI literature may be stronger in diagnosis than in implementable institutional/technical alternatives.
2. Solution-oriented work may live mainly in adjacent literatures: public infrastructure, industrial policy, commons governance, privacy-preserving systems, open hardware, digital public goods, and community/Indigenous governance.
3. `Sovereignty` should not be treated as synonymous with state control; competing forms include state, regional, community, Indigenous, individual, public-institutional and commons-based sovereignty/autonomy.
4. AI dependency is layered: chips, fabrication, accelerators, compute, cloud, software stacks, models, data, labor, distribution, standards and governance.
5. A system can appear “national” or “local” while retaining deep upstream operational dependencies (e.g. CUDA/toolchain/chip/model dependencies).
6. Infrastructure concentration and application/platform concentration operate through different mechanisms and should not be analyzed as one monopoly problem.
7. The global topology may be better modeled as polycentric/multi-bloc dependency rather than a simple US/West vs Global South binary.
8. A useful practical concept to investigate may be **exit capacity / substitutability / portability**, not full autarkic sovereignty.
