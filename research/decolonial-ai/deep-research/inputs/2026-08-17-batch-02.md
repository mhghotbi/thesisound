# Purpose

This batch is a structured capture of a second Deep Research result on AI power, coloniality, dependency, sovereignty, and alternative infrastructure. It is materially stronger than batch 01 because it separates causal literatures that had previously been conflated and explicitly distinguishes diagnosis from alternatives.

This file is **not** the canonical map. It preserves the synthesis, key distinctions, anchor works, and claims worth verifying before final consolidation.

# Executive field map proposed by the agent

The report argues there is no single coherent literature called “AI colonialism” that covers the whole problem. Instead, the field is composed of at least eight overlapping but distinct literatures:

1. **Postcolonial / Decolonial STS & Computing**
   - Core question: how technologies reproduce coloniality, hierarchy, and epistemic domination.
   - Status claimed: established independent tradition.

2. **Critical Data Studies: Data / Digital Colonialism**
   - Core question: how datafication turns social life, territories, and relations into resources for appropriation and domination.
   - Status claimed: recognizable but internally contested literature.

3. **Political Economy of Digital / AI Capitalism**
   - Core question: ownership, rents, monopoly, platform power, and intellectual monopoly.
   - Key point: should not be collapsed into infrastructure studies.

4. **Infrastructure & Material Political Economy of AI**
   - Core question: control over cloud, compute, semiconductors, energy, labor, and the industrial AI stack.
   - Key point: increasingly central after the rise of foundation models and hyperscale AI.

5. **Dependency, Development & Technological Capability**
   - Core question: how asymmetric endogenous capability reproduces center–periphery relations.
   - Key point: technological dependency is not synonymous with monopoly or foreign ownership.

6. **IPE, Geoeconomics & Weaponized Interdependence**
   - Core question: when network centrality, supply-chain chokepoints, and control over infrastructure become instruments of coercion.

7. **Digital / Data / Technological Sovereignty**
   - Core question: who should have meaningful control over data, infrastructure, and technological capability.
   - Key point: sovereignty is a response/agency literature, not simply the inverse of dependency.

8. **Commons, Public Infrastructure & Community Governance**
   - Core question: alternative ownership and governance arrangements for compute, data, models, and platforms.
   - Status claimed: family of solution-oriented literatures rather than a single discipline.

# Most important corrections to the earlier taxonomy

## 1. Political economy ≠ infrastructure ≠ dependency ≠ sovereignty

The report strongly argues that these should remain analytically separate:

- **Political economy** asks how accumulation, ownership, rents, IP, and market power work.
- **Infrastructure studies** asks where material and organizational dependencies are embedded.
- **Dependency theory / development literature** asks how unequal capabilities are reproduced structurally.
- **Sovereignty** concerns agency, control, capacity, jurisdiction, and responses to dependence.

These clusters overlap empirically but do different explanatory work.

## 2. Epistemic / linguistic coloniality is important but probably not a standalone field

The report treats epistemic, linguistic, and cultural coloniality in AI as an important interface among Decolonial AI, Critical Data Studies, NLP, and language-technology research, rather than as a mature independent canon.

Candidate cases / anchors named:
- Masakhane
- decolonising speech technology
- low-resource moderation / NLP work

## 3. Big Tech–military power is an interface, not necessarily a coherent standalone tradition

It is better located at the intersection of:
- Security Studies
- military innovation
- political economy
- cloud / infrastructure studies

## 4. Public compute / Public AI / digital commons is a convergence zone

It should not be treated as one unified literature. It gathers work from:
- public infrastructure policy
- commons governance
- cooperative governance
- industrial policy
- competition / interoperability
- public administration

## 5. Two missing cross-cutting literatures

The report says the earlier taxonomy underweighted:

### Standardization / private rule-making
Standards can shape:
- interoperability
- lock-in
- market access
- technical defaults
- certification
- international influence

### Development / technological capability
This literature helps distinguish merely having access to technology from possessing endogenous technological capability.

# Proposed genealogy

The report reconstructs the field roughly as follows:

1. **Dependency Theory (1960s–1970s)**
   - center–periphery
   - technological dependence
   - machinery / capital goods / advanced technology dependence
   - key anchor: Theotonio Dos Santos

2. **Coloniality (around 2000 and earlier roots)**
   - continuation of colonial matrices of power, classification, labor, knowledge, and Eurocentrism beyond formal colonial rule
   - key anchor: Aníbal Quijano

3. **Postcolonial Computing / technoscience (2000s–2010s)**
   - technology transfer and design are not neutral
   - historical, epistemic, developmental, and translation asymmetries matter
   - key anchor: Irani et al.

4. **Datafication / platform / extraction literature (2010s)**
   - data colonialism
   - digital colonialism
   - coloniality of data
   - Global South / South(s)

5. **Materiality of AI (2018–2021)**
   - minerals
   - logistics
   - labor
   - energy
   - data
   - institutions
   - key anchors: Crawford & Joler; Kate Crawford

6. **Foundation-model / hyperscale era (2020s)**
   - compute intensity
   - accelerator scarcity
   - cloud dependence
   - vertical ties between model firms and hyperscalers
   - model training cost concentration
   - semiconductor chokepoints

The report’s central historical claim is that older concepts remain useful but have become insufficient on their own:

- `data colonialism` sees appropriation through data but not GPU scarcity / semiconductor fabrication well.
- `platform capitalism` and `intellectual monopoly` explain ownership and rent but not geopolitical chokepoint coercion well.
- `Dependency Theory` explains capability asymmetry but not epistemic harms with the same granularity.
- `Weaponized Interdependence` explains coercion through hubs / chokepoints but is not a theory of colonial history, labor exploitation, or epistemic domination.

## 2. Infrastructural / dependency power

- standards

- Dependency / Development
- cloud–AI industrialization

## 3. Geopolitical / coercive power
Mechanisms:
- export controls
- sanctions
- denial
- chokepoints
- techno-blocs

Primary literatures:
- IPE
- Weaponized Interdependence

## 4. Epistemic / constitutive power
Mechanisms:

Primary literatures:

The report argues that labor, minerals, and energy are not best treated as a fifth dimension of power; instead, they form material substrates across all four dimensions.

# AI as a dependency stack

A useful stack proposed in the report:

`minerals / energy / logistics → semiconductors → compute → cloud → foundation models → data → human labor → platforms / applications → standards / governance`

For future synthesis, each layer should be analyzed along at least these variables:

- concentration
- foreign dependence
- substitutability
- switching cost
- capability ownership
- jurisdiction
- vulnerability to denial
- coercibility
- public / private ownership
- epistemic consequences

Important distinction from this batch:

> **Concentration and dependency are related but are not synonyms.**

A country or organization may face severe dependency even where there is more than one supplier, if all suppliers rely on the same small set of chip architectures, cloud interfaces, standards, software stacks, or upstream dependencies.

# Colonialism vocabulary — proposed boundaries

## Colonialism
Historical conquest / rule / expropriation and hierarchical control over populations and territories. Digital use may be comparative or analogical and is contested.

## Coloniality
Continuation of matrices of power, classification, knowledge, labor, and hierarchy after formal colonial administration.

## Postcolonial
Analytical tradition focused on afterlives of empire, histories, subjectivities, knowledge, representation, and power.

## Decolonial
A related but distinct tradition foregrounding modernity/coloniality, epistemic alternatives, and self-determination.

## Digital colonialism
Often used for domination through architecture and ownership of digital ecosystems: hardware, software, connectivity, platforms.

## Data colonialism
Appropriation of social and human life through data relations as a new logic of accumulation / social ordering.

## Algorithmic colonization / algorithmic coloniality
External imposition of algorithmic systems, values, and technological dependencies onto historically peripheral contexts.

## AI colonialism
The report’s assessment: **emerging umbrella label, not yet a stable independent canon**.

## Digital extractivism
Assessment: adjacent vocabulary crossing political ecology, data colonialism, Latin American critical theory, labor, and natural-resource extraction; probably not a coherent standalone literature.

# Important methodological warning about “colonialism”

The report insists that colonial language should only be used where a mechanism is specified. Candidate mechanisms include:

- expropriation without meaningful consent
- externally imposed rule / infrastructure
- racialized or spatially unequal labor hierarchy
- enforced technological dependency
- epistemic hierarchy
- territorial or mineral extraction
- suppression of local capability / autonomy

If evidence only shows that a foreign corporation is powerful, concepts such as `market power`, `dependency`, `infrastructural dominance`, or `extractivism` may be more precise than `colonialism`.

This is an important claim to verify later against critiques of Data Colonialism / Digital Colonialism.

# Global South — disaggregation proposed

The report warns against treating Global South as a single actor. It proposes analyzing cases according to mechanisms such as:

1. data extraction
2. outsourced / invisible labor
3. mineral and resource extraction
4. linguistic marginalization
5. epistemic marginalization
6. cloud / model dependency
7. regulatory / standards dependency
8. geopolitical vulnerability

It also highlights possible `platform sub-imperialism`: firms and states usually grouped inside the Global South may themselves reproduce hierarchy regionally.

For future case selection, a country should be characterized layer-by-layer rather than labeled simply “dependent” or “sovereign.”

# Sovereignty — proposed decomposition

## Digital sovereignty
Broad control over digital infrastructures, services, platforms, data, and rules.

## Technological sovereignty
Endogenous capability in critical technologies and industrial / technical capacity.

## Data sovereignty
Authority over collection, storage, access, reuse, and governance of data.

## Cloud sovereignty
Jurisdiction, operational control, continuity, privileged access, and reduced dependency at cloud layer.

## AI sovereignty
Emerging / unstable term usually combining:
- compute
- models
- data
- talent
- industrial capacity
- regulatory capacity

## Strategic autonomy
Broader ability to act despite external dependence or coercion; not limited to the digital sector.

## Main conceptual warning

`state sovereignty` is not the only sovereignty form.

Possible actors:
- state
- region
- public institution
- individual
- community
- Indigenous people / nation
- commons

The report makes two key critiques of state-centric sovereignty:

1. **Jurisdiction without capability may be sovereignty in name only.** Regulation does not remove dependency if compute, cloud, models, chips, or expertise remain external.
2. **Localization may only transfer the beneficiary of control.** Moving control from foreign corporations to domestic state/security institutions is not automatically decolonization.

# Alternatives — main families

The report evaluates alternatives by both technical and institutional components.

## Public compute / Computing Commons
Problem addressed:
- private hyperscaler dependence
- unequal compute access

Technical:
- shared GPU / HPC clusters
- allocation systems

Institutional:
- public funding
- eligibility / allocation governance

Maturity claim:
- medium–high due to initiatives such as NAIRR and EuroHPC

Key limitation:
- public facilities can still depend on private hardware, vendors, or cloud partnerships.

## Public AI
Problem:
- corporate control over model layer

Technical:
- public models
- datasets
- APIs
- evaluation / deployment stack

Institutional:
- public-service obligations and public institutions

Maturity claim:
- emerging / medium

## Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI)
Problem:
- proprietary dependence in core digital rails

Technical:
- interoperable public digital rails / APIs

Institutional:
- public / state governance

Warning:
- DPI is not inherently democratic or decolonial.

## Data commons / trusts / stewardship
Problem:
- corporate ownership and weak individual-consent models

Institutional forms:
- trusts
- stewards
- foundations
- fiduciary arrangements

Warning:
- should not be treated as a substitute for regulation; accountability remains fragmented.

## Indigenous Data Governance
Problem:
- extraction and loss of collective authority

Institutional principle:
- self-determination
- CARE
- community protocols

Strength:
- relatively mature institutional principles

Open question:
- scaling into foundation-model training and cross-jurisdiction systems.

## Open-source / open-weight AI
Strength:
- reduces API / model access dependency
- increases auditability and deployability

Core warning:
- `open weights ≠ full openness ≠ sovereignty`
- compute, data, deployment, chips, expertise, and infrastructure can remain concentrated.

## Platform cooperativism / Governable Stacks
Problem:
- concentrated platform ownership and governance

Institutional:
- member / community governance

Constraint:
- financing and scale against hyperscalers.

## Public / regional cloud and compute pooling
Problem:
- national-scale constraints

Technical:
- shared regional HPC / cloud infrastructure

Institutional:
- interstate / public consortia

Risks:
- unequal access within region
- vendor / hardware dependence
- governance complexity

## Industrial policy / capability building
Problem:
- structural technological dependency

Tools:
- fabs
- compute
- skills
- R&D
- procurement
- subsidy
- public investment

Main warning:
- buying equipment is not the same as building endogenous capability.

## Interoperability / portability / exit rights
Problem:
- switching cost and lock-in

Technical / policy tools:
- standardized APIs
- data portability
- model migration
- procurement rules

Important future question:
- how much portability is actually feasible where specialized accelerators and cloud-specific services are involved?

## Participatory / local-language AI
Problem:
- linguistic / epistemic dependence

Mechanisms:
- local datasets
- local evaluation
- local models
- participatory research communities

Warning:
- participation without ownership can still become extraction.

# Major debates to preserve for the canonical map

1. **Colonialism or capitalism?**
   - When does colonial framing explain more than general capitalist accumulation / monopoly?

2. **Sovereignty or openness?**
   - Openness without capability can be hollow; sovereignty can become nationalism / censorship.

3. **National autonomy or global commons?**
   - Full autarky is unrealistic, but deep interdependence can create weaponizable vulnerabilities.
   - Better question proposed: which dependencies are tolerable, substitutable, reciprocal, and non-coercible?

4. **Open source or public infrastructure?**
   - likely complements rather than substitutes.

5. **Regulation or ownership?**
   - should concentrated infrastructure merely be regulated, or in some cases publicly owned / provisioned?

6. **Decentralization or public centralization?**
   - physical compute may need scale and centralization while governance can still be plural / decentralized.
   - useful design problem: `centralized expensive hardware + decentralized/plural governance and allocation`.

7. **Open data or collective data sovereignty?**
   - open-science / FAIR logics can conflict with community authority and CARE principles.

## 4. Standards are under-integrated into decolonial AI

## 5. Labor and chip / compute literatures are siloed

# Nuanced assessment of the initial hypothesis

Initial hypothesis:

> Decolonial / Postcolonial scholarship is stronger at diagnosing AI power than at designing technical and institutional alternatives.

Batch 02 refines this as follows:

- **Mostly true at frontier-scale infrastructure level.** Decolonial work is not where GPU-cluster procurement, semiconductor industrial policy, cloud portability, or large-scale compute institutions are most developed.
- **Too crude as a general statement.** Indigenous Data Governance, CARE, participatory language technology, and Governable Stacks provide real institutional / design alternatives.
- The strongest infrastructure-scale solution literature often sits elsewhere:
  - public infrastructure policy
  - industrial policy
  - competition law
  - development economics
  - public administration
  - commons / cooperative governance
  - IPE / sovereignty

Proposed precise formulation to preserve:

> Decolonial/Postcolonial scholarship is especially strong at diagnosing historical, epistemic, and relational forms of AI power and contains serious community/data/design alternatives; however, the design of capital-intensive AI infrastructure and large-scale capability institutions is developed mainly in adjacent literatures such as public infrastructure, industrial policy, competition, development, and sovereignty.

# Provisional synthesis value

This batch contributes three especially useful ideas for the eventual canonical map:

1. **Infrastructure and dependency are the missing bridge between coloniality and sovereignty.**
2. **Dependency must be analyzed layer-by-layer, not inferred from market concentration alone.**
3. **The alternative question is not simply foreign Big Tech vs national sovereignty; ownership, capability, governance, exit, substitutability, and community authority are separate variables.**
