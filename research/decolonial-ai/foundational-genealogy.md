# Foundational Genealogy — From Anti/Postcolonial Thought to Decolonial Technology and AI

Status: **verified bridge map v1**
Date: 2026-08-17
Purpose: restore the upstream theoretical genealogy that sits behind the canonical technology/AI field map without turning the project into a generic survey of postcolonial theory.

This file is a **foundation layer**, not a seventh Thesisound research axis and not a reading list. Its job is to answer a narrower question:

> Which foundational anti-colonial, postcolonial, and decolonial concepts are actually upstream of the technology/AI literatures in this project, what did they originally mean, how have later technology scholars used them, and where would that transfer become misleading?

## 1. Structural decision: two genealogies, not one undifferentiated “postcolonial” canon

The project's technology literature inherits from at least two related but non-identical intellectual lineages.

### Genealogy A — anti-colonial / postcolonial critique

`Fanon → Said / Spivak / Bhabha → postcolonial technoscience / Postcolonial Computing → selected critical AI/data work`

Typical questions:
- How does domination shape the subject and psyche?
- Who has the authority to represent whom?
- How do knowledge and representation participate in imperial rule?
- How do colonial categories persist, mutate, or become ambivalent after formal empire?
- How can apparently universal technical/design systems carry situated histories and assumptions?

### Genealogy B — modernity/coloniality / decolonial thought

`Quijano → Mignolo / Walsh and the modernity-coloniality program → decolonial computing/design → Decolonial AI / colonial-matrix analyses of AI`

Typical questions:
- Why do colonial patterns of power persist after colonial administrations end?
- How are race, labor, knowledge, subjectivity, and modernity co-constituted through coloniality?
- How does Eurocentric universality organize legitimate knowledge?
- What might “delinking,” pluriversality, re-existence, or decolonial praxis mean?
- How can technical design reproduce or challenge the coloniality of power/knowledge/being?

### Why the separation matters

`coloniality` is not simply a new word for Said's `Orientalism`, and `decoloniality` is not just a chronological update of postcolonial studies. The modernity/coloniality school has a distinct Latin American genealogy and stronger structural claims about the persistence of colonial power beyond formal colonialism. Postcolonial theory is generally more suspicious of claims that one can recover pure precolonial epistemologies or identities; Spivak and Bhabha in particular complicate any simple restoration of an authentic subaltern voice or uncontaminated cultural essence.

For this project, the traditions should therefore be **connected but not collapsed**.

Evidence / useful sources:
- Quijano's foundational status in the modernity/coloniality program: Duke University Press, *Aníbal Quijano: Foundational Essays on the Coloniality of Power* (2024): https://www.dukeupress.edu/anibal-quijano
- Mignolo & Walsh, *On Decoloniality* (2018): https://www.dukeupress.edu/on-decoloniality
- scholarly discussion of differences/tensions between postcolonial and decolonial positions: https://doi.org/10.1080/13688790.2022.2041695

# 2. Foundation nodes

## F0. Frantz Fanon — colonial subjectivity, violence, decolonization, postcolonial elite

### Core works

- *Black Skin, White Masks* (1952)
- *The Wretched of the Earth* (1961)

Publisher/reference pages:
- https://groveatlantic.com/book/black-skin-white-masks/
- https://groveatlantic.com/book/the-wretched-of-the-earth/

### Original conceptual contribution relevant here

Fanon analyzes colonialism not only as territorial/economic rule but as a structure that enters subjectivity, racialized identity, language, recognition, social hierarchy, and psychic life. *The Wretched of the Earth* extends the analysis to decolonization, structural violence, national consciousness, and the dangers of post-independence elites reproducing dependent/neocolonial arrangements.

A reliable Oxford overview characterizes Fanon's theory as connecting economic, institutional, psychological, and racialized violence and emphasizes his warnings about distortions in postcolonial regimes and continuing exploitation after formal independence: https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780198717133.013.54

### Verified bridge into technology / AI

Mohamed, Png & Isaac's *Decolonial AI* (2020) explicitly invokes Fanon's psychodynamic perspective as one resource for decolonial AI analysis and connects contemporary labor/exploitation continuities to colonial histories: https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-020-00405-8

### What Fanon can legitimately contribute to this project

- understanding colonization/decolonization as transformations of power and subjectivity, not merely formal jurisdiction;
- caution that political independence or local ownership can coexist with dependent elite structures;
- analysis of paternalism and the production of colonized subjects;
- a strong warning against equating decolonization with simple replacement of foreign rulers by national elites;
- useful conceptual background for Axis 5's distinction between sovereignty and genuine self-determination.

### Misuse risk

Do **not** infer from Fanon that every technical dependency is colonial domination or that every localization/nationalization project is decolonization. Fanon's categories concern historically specific structures of racialized colonial rule and liberation. Application to AI must identify an actual mechanism and avoid metaphorical inflation.

### Relevance to axes

- Axis 2: MEDIUM
- Axis 3: MEDIUM
- Axis 4: MEDIUM
- Axis 5: **HIGH**
- Axis 6: MEDIUM, mainly as a warning against elite/state substitution

## F1. Edward Said — representation, discourse, knowledge/power, contrapuntal analysis

### Core works

- *Orientalism* (1978)
- *Culture and Imperialism* (1993)

Publisher pages:
- https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/159783/orientalism-by-edward-w-said/
- https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/159778/culture-and-imperialism-by-edward-w-said/

### Original conceptual contribution relevant here

Said's key problem is not simply that Western accounts of “the East” contain factual errors. His stronger claim is that systems of representation, scholarship, classification, and cultural production are entangled with material relations of imperial power. `Orientalism` names a durable system in which a dominant center acquires authority to define and represent an “Orient” as an object of knowledge.

*Culture and Imperialism* broadens that project and develops `contrapuntal reading`: reading metropolitan cultural forms together with the imperial worlds and suppressed histories that make them possible.

### Verified bridge into technology / AI

*Decolonial AI* explicitly recommends Said's `contrapuntal analysis` as a critical tactic for exposing binaries and codifications such as metropole/periphery and “West/rest” within AI research and products: https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-020-00405-8

This is a direct and unusually clean bridge from a canonical postcolonial thinker to contemporary AI scholarship.

### What Said can legitimately contribute to this project

- analyzing who gets to define categories, populations, risks, intelligence, language quality, benchmark validity, and “development”;
- asking how representations produced by dominant technical institutions become authoritative descriptions of others;
- reading an AI system/product together with the peripheral labor, resources, data, and histories excluded from its polished interface;
- distinguishing epistemic/representational power from simple ownership.

### Misuse risk

`Orientalism` should not become a synonym for any Western bias or any inaccurate model output. A Said-inspired claim is strongest when there is a durable representational system tied to institutions and asymmetric power, not merely isolated stereotype/error.

### Relevance to axes

- Axis 2: MEDIUM
- Axis 3: **VERY HIGH**
- Axis 4: LOW–MEDIUM
- Axis 5: MEDIUM
- Axis 6: MEDIUM via plural representation/design

## F2. Gayatri Chakravorty Spivak — subalternity, representation, epistemic violence, limits of speaking-for

### Core work

- “Can the Subaltern Speak?” (original 1988; revised version later incorporated into *A Critique of Postcolonial Reason*)

Columbia University Press's volume on the essay describes it as a major intervention joining Marxism, deconstruction, the international division of labor, and the problem of colonial representation: https://cup.columbia.edu/book/can-the-subaltern-speak/9780231143851/

### Original conceptual contribution relevant here

The central lesson for this project is **not** the simplified slogan “marginalized people have no voice.” Spivak's harder problem concerns the structural conditions through which speech becomes legible, represented, translated, heard, or overwritten within hegemonic systems of knowledge and political representation. Her work also warns that benevolent intellectuals can reproduce epistemic domination while claiming to recover or “give voice” to the oppressed.

### Verified bridge into technology

ICT4D scholarship has explicitly used Spivak to analyze representation, subjectivity, local history, and subaltern subjects in information-technology development: https://doi.org/10.1080/02681102.2017.1286284

Recent postcolonial IT-governance work likewise operationalizes Spivak around whether actors in the Global South can participate meaningfully in centralized IT decisions rather than merely being formally consulted: https://doi.org/10.1080/0960085X.2024.2325358

The bridge to AI is therefore plausible and evidenced, but less canonical/direct than Said → `contrapuntal analysis` or Quijano → `colonial matrix of AI`.

### What Spivak can legitimately contribute to this project

- evaluating claims that participatory AI “gives voice” to marginalized communities;
- distinguishing presence in a dataset/consultation from effective representational power;
- questioning who translates local needs into technical requirements and who retains interpretive authority;
- examining whether data collection listens to communities or simply makes them newly extractable/legible;
- a strong caution for Axis 6: `participation ≠ self-determination`.

### Misuse risk

Do not use `subaltern` as a generic synonym for poor, non-Western, minority, or low-resource user. Subalternity is a relational/structural position and Spivak's intervention is specifically hostile to easy intellectual claims to transparently recover “the authentic voice” of marginalized others.

### Relevance to axes

- Axis 2: HIGH
- Axis 3: **VERY HIGH**
- Axis 5: HIGH
- Axis 6: **VERY HIGH** for participatory/community governance evaluation

## F3. Homi K. Bhabha — hybridity, mimicry, ambivalence, cultural translation

### Core work

- *The Location of Culture* (1994)

Publisher page: https://www.routledge.com/The-Location-of-Culture/Bhabha/p/book/9780415336390

### Original conceptual contribution relevant here

Bhabha destabilizes simple colonizer/colonized binaries. Concepts such as `mimicry`, `ambivalence`, `hybridity`, `liminality`, and the `third space` describe cultural relations in which imposed forms are neither simply accepted nor cleanly rejected; they are translated, imitated, altered, and made unstable.

### Verified bridge into technology

Postcolonial information-systems scholarship has used Bhabha's concepts to study IT offshoring, corporate/open-source relations, and Global North–South IT governance. For example, a 2020 *Information Systems Journal* article explicitly locates Bhabha-inspired analysis in postcolonial computing/IT research: https://doi.org/10.1111/isj.12270

### What Bhabha can legitimately contribute to this project

- studying adoption/translation of imported technical systems without assuming either passive domination or authentic local independence;
- analyzing hybrid infrastructures, local adaptations, and institutional mimicry;
- challenging binary models such as `US stack vs sovereign/local stack` where actual systems are assembled from mixed dependencies and local practices.

### Misuse risk

For the project's core problems—compute concentration, export controls, cloud dependency, labor extraction—Bhabha is **not** a first-line causal framework. `Hybridity` can describe cultural/institutional translation but does not by itself explain ownership, bottlenecks, coercive leverage, or industrial capability.

### Relevance to axes

- Axis 1: LOW
- Axis 2: LOW–MEDIUM
- Axis 3: MEDIUM
- Axis 5: MEDIUM
- Axis 6: MEDIUM

**Decision:** retain as a **secondary foundation**, not a required anchor for every decolonial-AI source pack.

## F4. Aníbal Quijano — coloniality of power, Eurocentrism, persistence beyond formal colonialism

### Core works / concepts

Key formulations include:
- `coloniality`
- `coloniality of power`
- Eurocentrism as an ordering of knowledge and social classification

A recent Duke University Press collection identifies Quijano as foundational to the decolonial perspective and collects essays including “Coloniality of Power, Eurocentrism, and Latin America”: https://www.dukeupress.edu/anibal-quijano

### Original conceptual contribution relevant here

Quijano's crucial move is to distinguish historical `colonialism` from `coloniality`: long-lived patterns of power, racial/social classification, labor organization, knowledge, and subjectivity that survive the formal end of colonial administrations and are constitutive of modernity.

This is one of the most important upstream concepts for this entire project because it supplies a mechanism for asking about colonial continuities **without claiming that contemporary AI firms literally reproduce nineteenth-century colonial administration**.

### Verified direct bridge into AI

Two strong bridges are verified:

1. Mohamed, Png & Isaac, *Decolonial AI* (2020), explicitly grounds algorithmic coloniality in the `coloniality of power`: https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-020-00405-8

2. Muldoon & Wu, “Artificial Intelligence in the Colonial Matrix of Power” (2023), explicitly uses Quijano's colonial matrix as its core analytic and traces it through data collection, training, labor/material relations, and AI epistemologies: https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-023-00687-8

### What Quijano can legitimately contribute to this project

- distinguishing colonial continuity from simple present-day foreignness;
- linking economic/material hierarchy with knowledge and social classification;
- framing AI as part of a longer modernity/coloniality structure rather than an isolated technological disruption;
- asking whether access to AI reproduces a division between rule/model/infrastructure producers and dependent users;
- grounding Axis 3's `epistemic power` in a deeper theory of knowledge hierarchy.

### Misuse risk

`Coloniality` is an ambitious macro-theoretical concept. It should not become an unfalsifiable explanation for every asymmetry in AI. For specific empirical questions, political economy, labor studies, infrastructure studies, dependency analysis, or IPE may provide more precise causal accounts.

### Relevance to axes

- Axis 1: HIGH as historical/structural framing, not sufficient empirical mechanism
- Axis 2: **VERY HIGH**
- Axis 3: **VERY HIGH**
- Axis 4: MEDIUM
- Axis 5: HIGH
- Axis 6: HIGH as evaluative background

**Decision:** Quijano is a **core foundational anchor** for this project, not an optional historical detour.

## F5. Walter Mignolo & Catherine Walsh — modernity/coloniality/decoloniality, delinking, pluriversality, praxis

### Core works

- Walter Mignolo, *The Darker Side of Western Modernity* (2011): https://www.dukeupress.edu/the-darker-side-of-western-modernity
- Walter Mignolo & Catherine Walsh, *On Decoloniality: Concepts, Analytics, Praxis* (2018): https://www.dukeupress.edu/on-decoloniality

### Original conceptual contribution relevant here

This strand develops Quijano's framework toward `decoloniality` as praxis: challenging the universality of Western modernity, emphasizing geopolitics of knowledge, `delinking` from the colonial matrix, pluriversality, and forms of resistance/re-existence.

Walsh is especially useful for preventing the project from treating decoloniality as merely an academic label: *On Decoloniality* explicitly frames decoloniality as thinking-and-doing with local histories, struggles, and praxis.

### Verified bridge into technology / AI

- *Decolonial AI* explicitly cites Mignolo in its account of coloniality and its goal of plural epistemologies: https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-020-00405-8
- Cruz's “Decolonizing Philosophy of Technology” (2021) takes coloniality of power/knowledge/being into technical design and distinguishes top-down philosophical decolonization from bottom-up co-construction with marginalized groups: https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-021-00489-w

### What this node can legitimately contribute to this project

- evaluating whether “sovereign AI” changes underlying epistemic/technical relations or merely nationalizes them;
- conceptual vocabulary for plural technical futures rather than one universal development path;
- a bridge from diagnosis to design/praxis;
- basis for asking whether alternatives permit communities to define problems, values, and technical forms rather than merely consume localized versions of dominant systems.

### Misuse risk

`Delinking` should not be casually translated into technical autarky, disconnection from global networks, or “build every layer locally.” It is primarily a critique of epistemic/political subordination and a project of decolonial reorientation. Any translation into compute/cloud/chip policy requires separate industrial and technical analysis.

### Relevance to axes

- Axis 3: HIGH
- Axis 5: **VERY HIGH**
- Axis 6: **VERY HIGH**
- Axis 1/4: contextual rather than sufficient causal explanation

# 3. Bridge literature: where foundations actually enter technology

The upstream canon becomes relevant to Thesisound only through literatures that translate it into questions about design, infrastructure, data, and AI.

## B1. Postcolonial Computing

### Anchor 1
Lilly Irani, Janet Vertesi, Paul Dourish, Kavita Philip & Rebecca Grinter, “Postcolonial Computing: A Lens on Design and Development,” CHI 2010.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/1753326.1753522
- metadata: https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/postcolonial-computing-a-lens-on-design-and-development/

### Anchor 2
Kavita Philip, Lilly Irani & Paul Dourish, “Postcolonial Computing: A Tactical Survey,” *Science, Technology, & Human Values* 37(1), 2012.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/0162243910389594

### Significance

This is the most direct bridge from postcolonial theory/technoscience into computing. The 2012 article argues that postcolonial science studies should not merely add “cultural context” to existing technical questions; it can generate different questions and tactical ways of rereading/reimagining hegemonic computing scripts.

**Project implication:** Postcolonial theory should not be a decorative introductory chapter placed before “real” AI analysis. Its strongest role is to change what counts as a problem, whose categories are accepted, and how design/development relations are read.

## B2. The colonial impulse in universal technical visions

Paul Dourish & Scott Mainwaring, “Ubicomp's Colonial Impulse,” UbiComp 2012.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/2370216.2370238
- author/institution metadata: https://isr.uci.edu/content/ubicomps-colonial-impulse.html

The paper links ubiquitous computing's universalizing ambition to a colonial intellectual tradition and emphasizes the `locatedness` of innovation.

**Project implication:** a useful bridge for Axis 3 and Axis 6 whenever AI products claim universal subjects, universal categories, or a single inevitable technological trajectory.

## B3. Decolonial AI

Shakir Mohamed, Marie-Therese Png & William Isaac, “Decolonial AI: Decolonial Theory as Sociotechnical Foresight in Artificial Intelligence,” *Philosophy & Technology* 33, 659–684 (2020).
- https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-020-00405-8

This is a critical bridge because it does **not** draw from only one foundation. It explicitly combines:
- Quijano/Mignolo-style coloniality;
- Said's contrapuntal analysis;
- Fanonian psychodynamic perspectives;
- dependency/metropole–periphery analysis;
- Postcolonial Computing;
- Critical Technical Practice.

It therefore demonstrates why this project's foundational layer should be plural rather than “read Said, then apply him to AI.”

## B4. Colonial matrix of AI

James Muldoon & Boxi A Wu, “Artificial Intelligence in the Colonial Matrix of Power,” *Philosophy & Technology* 36, 80 (2023).
- https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-023-00687-8

This is currently one of the clearest direct routes from Quijano's macro-framework into contemporary AI. It explicitly analyzes AI through the colonial matrix of power and relates the framework to AI data, labor/material structures, knowledge epistemologies, and bottom-up alternatives.

---

## B5. Decolonial technical design / philosophy of technology

Cristiano Cordeiro Cruz, “Decolonizing Philosophy of Technology: Learning from Bottom-Up and Top-Down Approaches to Decolonial Technical Design,” *Philosophy & Technology* 34, 1847–1881 (2021).
- https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-021-00489-w

This bridge matters especially for Axis 6 because it asks what a **decolonial technical design practice** might look like, rather than stopping at critique. It stresses dialog with marginalized groups, co-construction, agency, and the difference between top-down intellectual decolonization and bottom-up sociotechnical practice.

# 5. What is core versus optional for this project?

## Tier A — required conceptual foundations

These directly repair gaps in the current AI/dependency map:

1. **Quijano — coloniality of power / knowledge**
2. **Said — representation / contrapuntal analysis**
3. **Spivak — subalternity / representation / epistemic violence**
4. **Fanon — decolonization, subjectivity, postcolonial elite / neocolonial reproduction**

These four should be understood before using `coloniality`, `epistemic domination`, `voice`, `decolonization`, or `self-determination` casually in later Thesisound work.

## Tier B — highly useful but problem-dependent

5. **Mignolo/Walsh** — essential when the research question moves from coloniality toward decolonial praxis, pluriversality, and alternatives.
6. **Bhabha** — valuable for hybridity/translation/adaptation, but not a core explanation of AI concentration or dependency.

## Tier C — broader canon not yet required

The project should **not** automatically expand into every canonical postcolonial thinker or text. Césaire, Glissant, Mbembe, Nandy, de Sousa Santos, Escobar, Wynter, and others may become important for specific research questions, but should enter through a documented need rather than canon-completion.

Reason: this is a research program about AI power/dependency and sociotechnical alternatives, not a survey course in postcolonial studies.

The previous `field-map.md` was not wrong to start its L4 anchor list with Postcolonial Computing and Decolonial AI; those are the most direct technology literatures. But it was **under-genealogized**.

Corrected interpretation:

```text
Fanon ─┬─ Said
       ├─ Spivak
       └─ Bhabha
            ↓

            ↓
```

Therefore the canonical `L4` node remains valid as a **technology-facing literature node**, but this file must be treated as its upstream genealogy.

# 7. Implications for the six Thesisound research axes

The foundational layer should **not** become Axis 0 with a mandatory theory-history episode sequence. Instead, every later deep dive should draw only the foundations necessary for its causal question.

### Axis 1 — AI capability stack & structural dependency
Primary foundations: Quijano as framing only; otherwise dependency theory, infrastructure, political economy dominate.

### Axis 2 — extraction & hidden labor
Primary foundations: Quijano + Fanon; Said/Spivak selectively. Colonial framing must be tested against labor/political-economy explanations.

### Axis 3 — language, knowledge, benchmarks & standards
Primary foundations: **Said + Spivak + Quijano**. This is where canonical postcolonial/decolonial theory has the highest direct value.

### Axis 4 — weaponized interdependence / coercion
Primary foundations: mostly IPE/security. Fanon/colonial history may contextualize but should not replace network/coercion analysis.

### Axis 5 — sovereignty / self-determination / managed interdependence
Primary foundations: **Fanon + Quijano + Mignolo/Walsh**, with Spivak/Bhabha as correctives against state-centric or essentialist notions of authentic sovereignty.

### Axis 6 — alternatives
Primary foundations: **Spivak + Mignolo/Walsh + decolonial technical-design literature**. The key test is whether an alternative redistributes problem-definition, authority, capability, and exit capacity—not whether it merely carries the label `local`, `open`, or `sovereign`.

# 8. Strong cautions to preserve downstream

1. **Postcolonial ≠ decolonial.** They overlap and interact, but have distinct genealogies and sometimes conflicting assumptions.
2. **Anti-colonial ≠ postcolonial.** Fanon is upstream of later postcolonial theory but should not be retroactively flattened into the later academic field.
3. **Colonialism ≠ coloniality.** Historical domination and its persistent structural logics are related but not interchangeable.
4. **Representation ≠ model accuracy.** Said/Spivak concern institutional and epistemic authority, not merely technical error rates.
5. **Subaltern ≠ minority.** Structural non-hearing/non-representation is stronger than demographic marginality.
6. **Decolonization ≠ localization.** Local hosting, a national model, or data localization can reproduce the same power relation or create new domestic coercion.
7. **Delinking ≠ autarky.** Do not translate epistemic/political decolonial vocabulary into full-stack national self-sufficiency without separate technical/economic argument.
8. **Hybridity ≠ sovereignty.** Hybrid adoption describes a relation; it does not establish capability, autonomy, or democratic control.
9. **Using a decolonial author does not make an argument empirically true.** Each contemporary AI claim still requires mechanism-specific evidence.

## Result

The project now has three linked levels:

```text
FOUNDATIONAL GENEALOGIES
anti/postcolonial + modernity/coloniality
        ↓
CANONICAL FIELD MAP
L / S / P / R / C
        ↓
SIX THESISOUND RESEARCH AXES
```

This prevents both failure modes:
- **theory amnesia**: using `coloniality/decolonial/epistemic` without understanding their conceptual ancestry;
- **theory domination**: forcing contemporary technical, industrial, labor, and geopolitical mechanisms into a classic postcolonial vocabulary when more precise literatures exist.
