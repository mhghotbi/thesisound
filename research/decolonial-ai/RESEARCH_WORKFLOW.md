## Quality rules

1. Separate intellectual traditions/literatures, AI system layers/sites, mechanisms/forms of power, responses/alternatives, and case/geopolitical positions.
2. Preserve disagreement instead of averaging agent outputs.
3. Prefer causal and conceptual precision over a visually neat taxonomy.
4. Distinguish established terminology from emerging or synthetic terminology.
5. Verify primary/authoritative sources before canonical promotion.
6. Separate descriptive diagnosis from normative proposal.
7. Separate technical mechanisms from institutional/governance mechanisms.
8. Avoid treating `Global South`, `sovereignty`, `colonialism`, `open source`, `decentralization`, `postcolonial`, or `decolonial` as internally uniform categories.
9. Current market figures and policy programs are time-sensitive and must be verified separately from conceptual literature.
10. Record reasons for every structural decision that affects the final map.
11. Do not use a classic theorist as a substitute for mechanism-specific empirical evidence.
12. Do not force every contemporary AI problem into postcolonial/decolonial vocabulary when political economy, labor studies, infrastructure studies, dependency theory, or IPE provides a more precise explanation.

## Step 4 — Canonical field map
Status: **COMPLETE**
Outputs:
- `research/decolonial-ai/field-map.md`
- `synthesis/step-04-canonicalization.md`

Canonical structure:
- orientation/core problem
- `L` literature map
- named second-order interfaces
- `S` stack/sites
- `P` power mechanisms
- `R` response families
- `C` case-position overlay
- cross-map relations
- debates/cautions/scope limits

Key decisions and reasons:

1. **No single-cluster taxonomy.** Mixing literature/site/mechanism/response caused the initial inconsistency.
2. **Political Economy remains separate from Infrastructure.** Ownership/rents/accumulation are not identical to material-organizational dependency.
3. **Dependency remains separate from Sovereignty.** Dependency is diagnosis; sovereignty is response/agency vocabulary.
4. **Data/Digital Colonialism is a named second-order interface.** Established but narrower than a parent discipline.
5. **Labor and language remain central without becoming sibling parent fields.** Labor is primarily a site/substrate; linguistic/epistemic inequality is a site + power mechanism + linked literature.
6. **Standards is explicitly cross-cutting.** Standards can shape interoperability, market access, defaults, lock-in, and geopolitical influence without ownership.
7. **Military-tech remains cross-cutting.** It spans political economy, infrastructure, security, and coercion.
8. **Four power families retained, coercion split P3a/P3b.** Labor/materiality are sites; ownership, dependency, coercion, and epistemic constitution are mechanisms.
9. **Responses remain an umbrella, not a discipline.** Public Compute, Public AI, industrial policy, commons, interoperability, etc. come from different traditions.

Canonical distinctions preserved:
- concentration ≠ dependency
- dependency ≠ vulnerability
- vulnerability ≠ coercibility
- access ≠ endogenous capability
- open weights ≠ open source ≠ public infrastructure ≠ sovereignty
- public ownership ≠ democratic governance
- localization ≠ sovereignty
- state sovereignty ≠ Indigenous/community self-determination
- colonialism ≠ coloniality

Excluded as unverified/time-specific at this stage:
- current GPU/cloud market shares/capex
- exact global geography of RLHF/annotation labor
- Persian tokenizer penalties
- Gulf AI-hub claims
- compute-budget comparisons
- claims that SLMs, mesh, federated learning, RISC-V, or local inference create system-level sovereignty

## Step 6 — Foundational Genealogy & Concept Bridge
Status: **COMPLETE**
Output: `research/decolonial-ai/foundational-genealogy.md`

Trigger: after Step 5, audit revealed that the canonical map retained technology-facing Postcolonial Computing/Decolonial AI sources but had underrepresented their upstream foundational theory. The user explicitly authorized a corrective genealogy step before source-pack/episode work.

Goal: identify only the foundational anti/postcolonial and decolonial concepts that materially inform this project's technology/AI questions; map original meaning → verified technology/AI bridge → legitimate use → misuse risk → relevant research axes.

Completion criterion: MET.

### Key Step-6 decision: two genealogies, not one canon

A. Anti-colonial/postcolonial lineage:
`Fanon → Said / Spivak / Bhabha → postcolonial technoscience / Postcolonial Computing → technology/AI applications`

B. Modernity/coloniality/decolonial lineage:
`Quijano → Mignolo / Walsh → decolonial computing/design → Decolonial AI / colonial-matrix analyses of AI`

Reason: `postcolonial` and `decolonial` overlap but are not interchangeable traditions. `Coloniality` specifically belongs to the modernity/coloniality lineage, while Said/Spivak/Bhabha pose different problems around representation, subalternity, hybridity, and discourse.

### Foundation ranking for this project

**Tier A — required conceptual anchors**
- Aníbal Quijano — coloniality of power/knowledge; strongest structural foundation for persistence beyond formal empire
- Edward Said — representation, knowledge/power, contrapuntal analysis; strongest direct foundation for Axis 3
- Gayatri Spivak — subalternity, representation, epistemic violence, limits of speaking-for; crucial for participation/data/governance
- Frantz Fanon — colonial subjectivity, decolonization, postcolonial elite, structural/psychic violence; crucial for Axis 5 and anti-localization simplification

**Tier B — problem-dependent**
- Mignolo/Walsh — delinking, pluriversality, decoloniality-as-praxis; especially Axes 5/6
- Homi Bhabha — hybridity/mimicry/ambivalence; useful for mixed/localized adoption but weak for explaining chips/cloud/market chokepoints

**Tier C — not automatically added for canon completeness**
Césaire, Glissant, Mbembe, Nandy, de Sousa Santos, Escobar, Wynter, etc. may enter later only when a specific research question requires them.

Reason: Thesisound is studying AI power/dependency and sociotechnical alternatives, not building a survey course in postcolonial theory.

### Verified bridges recorded in the Step-6 artifact
- Postcolonial Computing (CHI 2010; ST&HV 2012)
- Dourish/Mainwaring's `Ubicomp's Colonial Impulse` (2012)
- Mohamed/Png/Isaac `Decolonial AI` (2020)
- Cruz `Decolonizing Philosophy of Technology` (2021)
- Muldoon/Wu `Artificial Intelligence in the Colonial Matrix of Power` (2023)

### Strong cautions added
- postcolonial ≠ decolonial
- anti-colonial ≠ postcolonial
- colonialism ≠ coloniality
- representation ≠ model accuracy
- subaltern ≠ generic marginalized/minority user
- decolonization ≠ localization
- delinking ≠ autarky
- hybridity ≠ sovereignty
- citing a decolonial theorist does not empirically establish a contemporary AI mechanism

### Structural consequence

`field-map.md` remains valid as the **technology-facing canonical field map**. `foundational-genealogy.md` is now its upstream foundation layer rather than a new sibling `L` node or a seventh research axis.

New three-level project structure:

```text
FOUNDATIONAL GENEALOGIES
anti/postcolonial + modernity/coloniality
        ↓
CANONICAL FIELD MAP
L / S / P / R / C
        ↓
SIX THESISOUND RESEARCH AXES
problem-oriented trajectories
```

Reason: this avoids both `theory amnesia` (using colonial/decolonial vocabulary without ancestry) and `theory domination` (forcing technical/industrial/geopolitical mechanisms into classic postcolonial language when more precise literatures exist).
